Scandinavia, about 400 CE

Hnefatafl is the game Vikings played before chess got to them. The two sides aren't even. One player has a King and a small guard, the other has twice the pieces and wants him caught.

How to play Hnefatafl

  1. The King starts on the throne in the middle with 12 defenders around him. 24 attackers line the edges.
  2. Defenders win if the King reaches any corner square. Attackers win if they surround the King on all 4 sides, or on 3 sides plus the throne.
  3. Every piece moves like a rook in chess, any number of squares in a straight line. No jumping.
  4. Only the King can stop on the throne or a corner.
  5. Capture a normal piece by trapping it between two of yours. The throne and corners count as enemies for this.

A little history

The name means "fist table" in Old Norse. Before chess came in from the Arab world around 1000 CE, tafl games were the strategy game of Northern Europe, played from Ireland to Ukraine.

Because the sides have different pieces and different goals, it plays a lot like a Viking raid, with one side trying to break out and the other closing in.
